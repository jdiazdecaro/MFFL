The columnSixteen teams, two leagues

A 94.9-point gap separates the best roster in this league from the worst. Every inch of it was built on purpose.

By The Beat · Staff report

The MFFL opens its fifth season Wednesday with a 94.9-point gap between its best roster and its worst. CeeDees TDs projects 201.1 points a week. Loud and Stroud projects 106.3. The schedule did not do that. Sixteen managers did, one trade at a time, over five months.

The split is clean, and you can read it right off the table. Every one of the top eight rosters has 31% or less of its value tied up in players under 24. All four of the bottom four have 48% or more. Young teams are bad this year. Old teams are good. There is barely an exception in the league. The top four average 188.6 points a week. The bottom four average 118.5. That is a 70.1-point gap inside one league. The middle sits around 147.8, and the teams parked there are the ones that did the least all summer.

Two managers pushed it to the limit. CeeDees TDs has 1% of its value under 24. The People’s Champ has 0%. Both sold the future outright and will be judged in January. Blue Tent All-Stars went the other way at 59%, more of its money in young players than anyone else in the league, and projects 123.8. Both plans are coherent. Neither is comfortable.

The strange one is in the middle. The Comeback Kid made 7 of the league's 14 trades, half of them by himself, and projects 15th at 107.6. He owns 1 of a possible four first-round picks in 2027 and 2028. Activity is not accumulation. Bay Area Party Supplies made 2 trades, said little, and owns 6 of those same firsts while projecting fifth.

The top of the league is the hurt part of it. All three start on Sunday. All three are knee or ankle.

Three teams carry 3 injured starters apiece: The People’s Champ, Bay Area Party Supplies and Demboyz. All three sit in the top nine, which is how a projection turns into a rumour by Sunday morning. Bay Area Party Supplies has the most injured players overall at 8. CeeDees TDs, Brute Force Attack and Loud and Stroud have the fewest, at 2 each.

Then there is the IR. 11 players sit on reserve slots, led by Zach Charbonnet (Knee - ACL, Child Support 💸) and Jayden Higgins (Knee - ACL, Blue Tent All-Stars). The second one stings: Jayden Higgins was the return in April's trade that sent T.J. Hockenson the other way, and he tore an ACL before playing a snap for his new roster.

A word of caution on all of this. A questionable tag in the first week of September is a soft signal. Some of these are precautionary, some are veterans on a rest schedule, and a few of the ACL notes are last season's injury still on the file rather than a new one. Read the table as a watch list, not a casualty list.

Off the wireFive pickups worth watching

Managers added 87 players off free agency and waivers since March. Only 35 are still on the roster that claimed them. Three of those start in week 1.

1. Darren Waller, TE, to The Comeback Kid. Claimed August 12 and up 678 in thirty days. He projects 13.5 points a week, more than any other player picked up off the wire in this league, and he starts at flex on Sunday. A 33-year-old tight end nobody wanted is now the 4th-highest projected scorer on the roster, and he cost nothing. Seven trades chased what a waiver claim delivered.

2. Chris Brooks, RB, to Bay Area Party Supplies. Claimed August 30, and he starts at running back in week 1 for the 5th-ranked roster in the league. He projects 6.8 a week. That is not a knock on Brooks. It is what a backfield looks like behind Ashton Jeanty.

3. Jalon Daniels, QB, to Child Support. Claimed August 25 and up 494, the biggest quarterback gain of anyone taken off the wire. He projects nothing yet. But this is a 2QB league where two teams cannot fill the ninth slot, and a free quarterback with a pulse is the most valuable thing on this list.

4. Tyreek Hill, WR, to The Comeback Kid. Claimed August 29 for nothing, the biggest name to change hands all summer without a trade. He projects zero points right now. Either the best free bet in the league or a roster spot burned on a name. Watch this one.

5. Jacob Saylors, RB, to Chosen one. Claimed August 31 and up 523, the sharpest riser added in the last two weeks. He projects 5.0 a week on the oldest roster in the league, which is the one place a cheap young back has somewhere to go.

Notes · Week one

1. Two teams cannot fill the superflex slot. One team has four quarterbacks. This is a 2QB league. The ninth starting spot takes a QB, and 14 of 16 teams put one there. Loud and Stroud does not have a second startable quarterback, so the slot goes to Devaughn Vele, a receiver, for 10.4 points. Do it to them uses Aaron Jones, a running back, for 12.8. The typical team gets 18.7 from that slot. That is 8.3 and 5.8 points a week, every week, for fourteen weeks. Remember the raiders is carrying 4 startable quarterbacks and can play two. The deadline is week 9.

2. The tight end premium is running this league. MFFL pays a tight end 0.6 per catch and a full point per first down. Managers noticed. 9 of 16 teams project to start two or more tight ends in week 1, and Blue Tent All-Stars and The Comeback Kid start three. It is why The Comeback Kid, 15th overall, still has the best tight end room in the league at 54.0 points a week. And why Loud and Stroud's 12.1 costs so much.

3. The market moved hard on the rookies. Biggest 30-day riser on any roster: Mike Washington, RB for SlickBears, up 877. Biggest faller: Cam Ward, QB for Vick2times, down 712. Four of the six steepest declines are players drafted in the last two rookie classes. September is when camp hype meets the depth chart.

MethodHow to read this

Projected points per week
Each team's best legal lineup (QB, RB, RB, WR, WR, TE, FLEX, FLEX, SUPERFLEX) scored against Sleeper's weekly 2026 projections, averaged over weeks 1 to 14. It assumes every manager starts their best lineup every week. Nobody does. Read it as roster strength, not a points forecast.
Scored under MFFL settings, not standard PPR
This league pays +0.3 per completion, −0.3 per incompletion, +1.0 per rushing first down, and gives tight ends +0.6 per catch and +1.0 per first down. Points here come from raw projected stats run against those settings. Sleeper's pts_ppr field ignores all of it and misranks quarterbacks and tight ends in this league.
Roster value and under-24 share
Dynasty trade values from FantasyCalc, set to superflex and full PPR, then adjusted by position for MFFL scoring (QB ×1.14, RB ×1.43, TE ×1.24, WR ×1.00). Under-24 share is the percentage of a roster's adjusted value held by players under 24. It measures how a team is built, not how good it is.
Why not a 16-team value source
No public source is built for a 16-team superflex league with this scoring, so this page says which compromise it made. FantasyCalc gets superflex right, which is by far the setting that matters most here, but it caps at 14 teams: ask for 16 and it returns the 12-team table unchanged, re-verified September 9 across all 423 players.

The cost is small. Moving from 12 to 14 teams shifts the median player +0.61%, and not one player in 423 moves more than five places in the overall order. RotoTrade does support 16 teams, but only inside its per-trade analyzer; its published value chart is 1QB, which prices Josh Allen outside the top 25 and would be far more wrong here. The young-versus-old split at the top of this page was checked against that second source anyway, and holds either way.
The superflex baseline
Fourteen of sixteen teams fill the ninth lineup slot with a quarterback. The median week 1 output from that slot among those fourteen is 18.7 points. The two teams that use a non-quarterback are measured against it.
What this page is not
No projected records, no playoff odds. Every number here comes from a projection, and September projections are the least reliable of the year. Depth charts move through final cuts, and one injury rewrites a capsule. Nothing here has watched a football game.
