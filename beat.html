The opening storyThe injury report became the standings.

Week 2 was supposed to tell the MFFL who was good. Instead, Sunday and Monday kept asking who was still available. Caleb Williams. Jayden Daniels. Jaxson Dart. Three young quarterbacks, three injury scares, and three managers suddenly reading the backup depth chart with much more interest. In a sixteen-team superflex league, that is a change to the competitive landscape.

Bay Area Party Supplies lost Williams to a right-hamstring injury Sunday. Chicago calls him week-to-week, and has not ruled him out for Week 3. Bay Area still scored 173.73—third in the league this week—and still lost. CeeDees brought 213.33. The box score hurt; the uncertainty at quarterback may last longer. Bears update.

TexasTimeshifts got the win and the worry. Daniels dislocated his elbow against Dallas; Washington reported no fracture on the initial X-rays and further testing ahead. His 20.60 helped Texas beat Loud and Stroud, 152.63–77.24. Marcus Mariota took over for Washington. That makes the next waiver cycle part of the story before the victory has even settled. Commanders recap.

Then Monday arrived. Dart injured his knee on the Giants’ opening possession, and Jameis Winston replaced him. Child Support received just 0.97 from its starting quarterback and finished 10.96 behind Demboyz. The next medical update matters for Week 3; the damage to Week 2 is already on the scoreboard. Giants injury update.

The receivers did not escape the week either. Puka Nacua was inactive with a hip injury, while Malik Nabers hurt his shoulder Monday before returning. The People’s Champ kept Nacua outside the starting nine and won; Brute Force survived Nabers’ 1.10. Same injury-heavy week, different consequences depending on the bench and the opponent. Rams inactives; Monday recap.

Do it to them had its own Sunday worries: Jayden Reed exited with a neck injury, and Saquon Barkley was listed with a stinger. NFL injury roundup. Their combined 4.40 left little margin in a lineup that finished last for the week.

The useful detail amid the uncertainty: Bay Area already owns Tyson Bagent, and Child Support already owns Winston. Those managers have an option if their starters miss time. Texas does not own Mariota—and, at this snapshot, neither does anyone else. The week’s most consequential open roster spot may belong to a quarterback who was a backup on Sunday morning.

The waiver columnNext up on the wire

These are the potential top waiver adds for MFFL heading into Week 3.

The wire has already put points on the board. Offseason pickup Darren Waller has delivered 29.10 starting-lineup points for The Comeback Kid through two weeks. Fellow offseason add Kalif Raymond has scored 28.05, including 10.50 in Brute Force’s Week 2 starting lineup. More recently, Loud and Stroud’s $70 claim on Carson Wentz returned 6.37 starting points in Week 2. The payoff varies, but working the wire can turn an open roster spot into points that count.

1 · Marcus Mariota · QB, Washington · Available
The strongest immediate case. Daniels’ elbow injury puts Mariota in position to handle the offense while Washington evaluates its starter. TexasTimeshifts has the clearest need; other teams short a second quarterback should be interested too. Bid for the possibility of usable starts, while accepting that the length of the opening is uncertain. In MFFL, quarterback rushing first downs and completion scoring matter alongside touchdown upside. Washington’s report.
2 · Darius Slayton · WR, Indianapolis · Available
Reportedly joining Indianapolis. That makes him a low-cost watch-list add. A new team is an opening to investigate; it does not establish routes, targets or an immediate start. Wait for his practice and game role before putting him into a lineup.
3 · Tutu Atwell · WR, Los Angeles Rams · Available
A small, conditional claim if Nacua and Jordan Whittington remain unavailable. Both missed Monday, creating room in the receiver rotation. Atwell is on Sleeper’s Rams depth chart, but Davante Adams remains the established draw and Konata Mumpfield is already owned by CeeDees. This is a bet on a possible role, not a claim that Atwell has won one. Confirmed absences.
4 · Tahj Brooks · RB, Cincinnati · Available
A dynasty bench stash after The Comeback Kid dropped him Monday to add Zach Ertz. Sleeper lists Brooks third on Cincinnati’s running-back depth chart. He is not a verified injury replacement or an immediate fantasy starter. For a rebuilding team with an expendable bench spot, the low-cost backfield option is worth considering; a contender needing points now should keep looking.

The week on the fieldCeeDees made the big game look small.

The advertised matchup delivered 387.06 combined points. It also delivered a fairly decisive answer: CeeDees TDs 213.33, Bay Area 173.73. Every CeeDees starter reached 18.70. CeeDee Lamb led at 35.50, but the frightening part was the absence of a weak spot. Bay Area’s Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Amon-Ra St. Brown combined for 78.50 and still could not bridge the gap.

Chosen one. supplied the other heavyweight performance. Josh Allen’s 47.67 and Patrick Mahomes’ 32.23 powered a 191.00–70.23 win over Remember the raiders. The quarterback pair alone outscored the opposition. CeeDees and Chosen one. were the top two weekly scorers; both are 2–0.

Demboyz changed the conversation after a 90.90 opener, jumping to 166.30 and beating Child Support’s 155.34. Dak Prescott and Jared Goff combined for 66.80; Bateman added 21.80. Dart’s early exit hangs over the result, but Demboyz also put a far better lineup total on the board.

Five teams remain unbeaten: CeeDees, Chosen one., The People’s Champ, SlickBears and Blue Tent. Five are still looking for a first win. Two weeks have separated the records; the injury updates may determine how quickly those groups change.

Off the wireBoston changes hands. So does a first.

Blue Tent All-Stars received Denzel Boston and bLuE BaLLeRs’ 2027 second-round pick. The Comeback Kid received Emanuel Wilson and The People’s Champ’s 2027 first-round pick. Blue Tent adds a young receiver and retains a future selection; The Comeback Kid adds running-back depth and moves up a round in draft capital.

Elsewhere, Blue Tent added Devontez Walker, Chosen one. paid $10 for Colby Parkinson, and The Comeback Kid added Zach Ertz while dropping Tahj Brooks. Brooks returns to this week’s available-player pool. The deals and claims show two ways to address a thin roster: trade for a longer-term piece or search the wire for immediate help.
