The waiver column$276 later, who got better?

The Champ bought another running back. Loud and Stroud bought quarterback insurance. The rest of the league got the receipt.

By The Beat · MFFL desk

Wednesday’s waiver cycle turned into an early-Thursday auction, and The People’s Champ brought the biggest paddle. Raheim Sanders went for $111. Carson Wentz cost Loud and Stroud $70. Bay Area Party Supplies spent $44 on Devin Singletary. Six winning claims, $276 off the board, and Week 2 had yet to throw a punch.

The Champ paid for another card in an already loaded hand. Bijan Robinson, Jonathan Taylor, D’Andre Swift and Jaylen Warren supplied 116.10 in the opener. Sanders arrives as reinforcement, with Colby Parkinson making way. Bay Area’s failed $94 bid was the nearest challenger shown in the feed. A $17 winning margin is the cost of refusing to finish second.

Loud and Stroud bought a possibility. Carson Wentz belongs to Zaiah2000 now, but Minnesota’s starting job still depends on Kyler Murray’s progress through concussion protocol. The $70 bid beat unsuccessful offers of $31 from Brute Force Attack and $25 from Do it to them. That is a serious premium for a team that put Tre Tucker in superflex last week. Insurance gets expensive after the sirens start.

Bay Area took the smaller running-back pot. Singletary cost $44, three dollars above CeeDees TDs’ unsuccessful bid. The Comeback Kid offered $35. After scoring 197.20, Bay Area could have admired the view. Instead, it added depth before a date with CeeDees. The contender across the table had wanted the same player.

The quieter desks stayed busy. Brute Force landed Noah Fant for $31, Remember the raiders bought Kendrick Bourne for $10, and SlickBears added Demarcus Robinson for $10. Blue Tent All-Stars followed with Mack Hollins; CeeDees added DeeJay Dallas. Small names, real roster spots, and in a sixteen-team league the supporting cast gets speaking lines.

The Beat’s read: Loud and Stroud made the move that could most visibly change a lineup—if Wentz gets the start. Bay Area added depth ahead of the week’s marquee game. The Champ paid the largest bill without needing Sanders to rescue its starting nine. None has bought a Week 2 point yet. The receipts are real; the returns arrive Sunday.

Matchup of the weekBay Area vs. CeeDees: ring the bell

Two unbeaten teams. A combined 377.67 points in Week 1. Somebody’s opening statement is about to meet cross-examination.

Bay Area Party Supplies (1–0) vs. CeeDees TDs (1–0). Bay Area brings the league’s second-highest opening score, 197.20. CeeDees brings 180.47 and the preseason model’s No. 1 ranking. This is the game that gets the big type. Week 1 gave both managers something to brag about; Week 2 puts them in the same room.

First bell: four quarterbacks, one substantial argument. Caleb Williams and Drake Maye combined for 61.00 for Bay Area. Lamar Jackson and Jalen Hurts supplied 53.77 for CeeDees. MFFL pays for completions and takes points back for incompletions, so passing volume alone will not settle this. Efficiency has a seat at ringside.

Second bell: Jeanty against the three-headed backfield. Ashton Jeanty scored 39.70. CeeDees countered with Jahmyr Gibbs at 45.60, plus Kyren Williams and Christian McCaffrey. Those three totaled 78.90. Bay Area currently has TreVeyon Henderson in for Chris Brooks, whose opening contribution was 3.90. That second running-back slot is one place the challenger can make up ground.

Third bell: the receivers behind the marquee. Bay Area’s four starting wideouts combined for 80.50. CeeDees got 44.00 from its three, including Jordan Addison’s zero. Bay Area’s receiver depth carried real weight in Week 1; CeeDees has room for a rebound without asking Gibbs to deliver another masterpiece.

The deciding question: can Bay Area’s receivers offset CeeDees’ running-back punch for a second straight week? Those Week 1 position totals describe how the teams won, not a forecast. The current lineups can still change. For now, this is the best matchup on the card—and a fine time to keep the group chat open.
