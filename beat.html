The opening story$190 for a chance. Time to choose.

Carlos buys the biggest ticket on a $220 waiver night. With the October 21 trade deadline ahead, the league has less than two weeks to decide what this season is worth.

Do it to them is 0–4. Carlos is still buying. The overnight waiver run sent Tyler Huntley to Carlos for $190, with Andy Dalton dropped. That one claim accounted for 86.4% of the league’s $220 spending. Four completed claims cleared just after midnight Pacific on October 8. The other three cost $10 apiece.

Huntley is a conditional bet on quarterback starts. Lamar Jackson missed Thursday practice, and Huntley is the expected replacement if Jackson cannot play. Carlos paid for that opening; its length remains uncertain. In a sixteen-team superflex league, even a temporary second quarterback can matter. Jackson update · Oct. 8. Carlos has $446 remaining in the refreshed budget snapshot.

That leaves less than two weeks to buy help, move veterans for future capital, or keep the roster together. The Beat’s read: Carlos’s bid is a move to compete now, not proof that a turnaround is coming. At the top, Nick and Fristoe can ask what a title push is worth. Beyond the recordBrute Force is better than 1–3 looks.

Eddie’s Brute Force Attack is fifth in season scoring and twelfth in the record-first standings. His team’s 554.93 points exceed the totals of 3–1 TexasTimeshifts and SlickBears. The difference is the other column: opponents have scored a league-high 686.25 against Brute, or 171.56 per week.

Last week’s 2.78-point loss to Trey’s SlickBears makes the pressure tangible. Eddie gets Texas this week. A win would leave Brute 2–3 and Texas 3–2, pulling them within one victory of each other. A loss creates a three-win separation. The scoring says this is worth more attention than a casual glance at the records gives it.

Prime Time’s Child Support is the other team the record undersells. At 2–2 with 676.26 points, his team is only 0.74 behind unbeaten Chosen one. in season scoring. That is why Blue Tent’s matching 2–2 record should not be mistaken for an identical opening month.

This week’s question: which catches up first—the results to Brute’s scoring, or the scoring to the records of the teams ahead? Four games are evidence, not a verdict.
