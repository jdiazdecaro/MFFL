Three weeks into the season, the question around MFFL is becoming familiar: who can actually play? Injuries come with fantasy football. But when starting quarterbacks, cornerstone receivers and lead running backs disappear from lineups together, the bench starts deciding matchups.

Having nine suitable starters is only the beginning. Managers need replacements—and increasingly, replacements for those replacements. Every MFFL roster carries at least one injury designation in this snapshot. Nobody gets to sit this conversation out.

Meanwhile, the standings have sharpened. CeeDees TDs, Chosen one. and The People’s Champ are 3–0. Four teams remain winless. Week 4 puts two unbeaten teams together and two winless teams together, guaranteeing movement at both ends unless those games finish tied.

The defending champion offers a useful lesson for this moment. Ger’s title roster took shape through connected trades, but it also found championship points in players picked up along the way. This week’s wire could supply somebody else’s next useful piece.

The Week 4 wireHelp that is actually available.

Four available players with a reason to look. Priorities depend on the hole in your lineup.

National waiver lists can feel like shopping lists for somebody else. Braelon Allen, Ollie Gordon, Kenyon Sadiq and Jalon Daniels are already rostered in MFFL. The four names below were available at the publication check; their availability can change.

There is no verified, freely available running back here to sell as a guaranteed Week 4 solution. That scarcity is part of the story. In this league, useful insurance often has to arrive before the opening.

Tyler Higbee TE · RamsImmediate tight-end help

Eight catches on 11 targets for 62 yards and a touchdown give the recommendation substance. With Terrance Ferguson managing an ankle injury, Higbee belongs at the top of the list for a manager missing a tight end. Monitor Ferguson’s progress rather than assuming the same workload repeats. Week 3 usage.

Case Keenum QB · BearsConditional superflex priority

A quarterback who might start again matters in a sixteen-team superflex league. Keenum started Monday with Caleb Williams sidelined, but Ben Johnson has not committed to his Week 4 starter. Tyson Bagent is back in the picture. For a QB-needy roster, Keenum can be the first claim; the bid should account for the uncertainty. Chicago’s quarterback decision.

Brycen Tremayne WR · PanthersDeep receiver claim

Four catches on six targets put Tremayne on the list as Carolina manages receiver injuries. That is enough to investigate an emergency role, not enough to declare a dependable weekly starter. A modest claim makes more sense than treating one game as a breakout. Deep-league waiver report.

Jordan Watkins WR · 49ersSpeculative depth

Two catches for 60 yards make Watkins worth a look in a depleted receiving group. His opportunity depends partly on who returns, including Evans. Add for a possible opening; watch the routes and targets before trusting him in a must-win lineup. Deep-league waiver report.

The road to a championshipHow Ger put the pieces together.

A 13–1 regular season. A decisive championship. More than a year of connected moves.

Ger beat Nick 192.14–109.67 in last season’s final. The stars delivered, but the roster around them had been shaped through trades and smaller additions well before championship week. With the two teams meeting again at 3–0, the timing is right to look back.

The ledger reveals a chain: the Taylor deal brought back a pick later used for Puka. DJ Moore followed the same path. Pearsall arrived and moved again the next day, bringing back two eventual championship starters. The timeline below follows those verified moves from 2024 onward; it is not a complete history of every player in Ger’s core.
