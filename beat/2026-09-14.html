The columnThe projections were too high. All of them.

Thirteen of sixteen teams scored under their preseason number. The team that beat it by the most was not supposed to be near the top.

By The Beat · Staff report

The MFFL averaged 127.27 points in week 1. The preseason model said 150.93. 13 of 16 teams finished under their own number. Some of that gap closes Monday night, but not most of it.

The week belonged to Bay Area Party Supplies, and almost nobody saw it coming. It scored 197.20, the most in the league, and it was projected 5th. Caleb Williams went for 41.07, the best quarterback game of the week. Ashton Jeanty added 39.70 with a knee tag on him all Saturday. The roster left 7.90 points on the bench. Only Demboyz left fewer, and Demboyz scored 90.90. Bay Area Party Supplies beat its preseason number by 33.90. Nobody else beat theirs by more than 11.37.

At the top, the favourites mostly held. CeeDees TDs scored 180.47 and won by 66.01. The People’s Champ scored 190.84 and won by 99.94, and did it while Ja’Marr Chase, the fourth most valuable player in this league, caught two balls for 3.20. That is what a deep roster is for.

The floor was worse than advertised. Six games were decided by more than 55 points. One was decided by less than ten. The closest game of the week is already final: Blue Tent All-Stars 107.86, Remember the raiders 101.70. Both teams have every starter locked. Everything else on the board was over by Sunday afternoon.

MethodHow to read this

Week 1 points
Actual points scored by the nine players each manager started, taken from this league’s own matchup results. Not a projection. Every total on this page is through Sunday night, September 13. Denver at Kansas City had not kicked off, so eight starting slots across five rosters were still empty. Those five teams are marked with a dagger.
Why the Sleeper app may show a slightly different total
Numbers here come from the league’s matchup results. The Sleeper app can display a team total up to 0.02 lower, because this league scores passing yards at 1 point per 30 and the app rounds that down at a different step. It only ever affects teams with a quarterback, and it is never enough to change a result: the closest two scores in week 1 are 0.07 apart.
Kick and punt return yards
Voted in by the competition committee and applied Friday, September 11, at 1 point per 20 yards. It is in every number on this page. It was worth 14.65 points across seven starting lineups in week 1.
Scored under MFFL settings, not standard PPR
This league pays +0.3 per completion, −0.3 per incompletion, +1.0 per rushing first down, +0.05 per kick or punt return yard, and gives tight ends +0.6 per catch and +1.0 per first down. A 25-point week here is not a 25-point week anywhere else, and Sleeper’s standard PPR field will not reproduce any number on this page.
Beat
How many of the other fifteen scores that team’s total would have beaten. A team can go 1 and 0 having beaten only six other scores, or 0 and 1 having beaten ten. It separates the week a roster had from the opponent it drew. Brute Force Attack beat ten of fifteen and lost. Blue Tent All-Stars beat six and won.
Bench points left
The best legal lineup available in hindsight, minus what the manager actually started. It is a scorekeeping number, not a verdict: nobody knows on Saturday which bench player was going to score 25. The league left 370.02 points on benches in week 1 and started 84.1% of what it had available.
Projected points per week
The preseason number published here on September 9: each roster’s best legal lineup scored against Sleeper’s weekly 2026 projections and averaged over weeks 1 to 14. It assumes a perfect lineup every week, so it runs high by design. The league came in 23.65 under it on average. Read the gap as a measure of the projection, not only of the team.
Under-24 share and average age
Carried forward unchanged from the September 9 edition: the percentage of a roster’s dynasty trade value held by players under 24, from FantasyCalc set to superflex and full PPR, then adjusted by position for MFFL scoring. It measures how a team is built. It is not a rating.
What this page is not
No projected records and no playoff odds. This is one week of results out of fourteen, and the single largest thing it proved is that projections in September are soft. Three teams beat their number. Thirteen did not. Check back in a month before deciding what any of it meant.
