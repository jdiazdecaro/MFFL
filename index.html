Draft Grades: All Sixteen

Eighty picks, five rounds, sixteen teams. Graded on the talent actually acquired, the value captured relative to where each player went, whether the picks addressed a real hole, and how many drafted rookies have a path to the field in 2026.

How these were graded

Talent
Total value of players acquired, from consensus rank, NFL depth-chart position, and age. Rewards absolute haul, not thrift.
Value over slot
Each player's value against what was typically available within eight picks either side. Measures whether you beat your own draft position.
Impact path
How many drafted rookies sit at NFL depth-chart 1 or 2 — the only ones with a realistic route to 2026 snaps.
Need fit
Pre-draft positional shortfall against a superflex lineup: two starting QBs, two RB, two WR, one TE, two flex.

Two honest limits. Consensus rank compresses everything outside the top ~250 into one undifferentiated bucket, so late-round grades separate players by depth chart and age rather than by talent. And August depth charts are noisy — they shift through final cuts. Treat the top of this list as firm and the bottom as directional.

01 RayRod94 A

The cleanest draft in the league. Fernando Mendoza at 1.02 is the single most valuable outcome available in a two-quarterback format — a top-40 rookie who is already his team's NFL starter, in the one position where this league forces you to start two. Makai Lemon adds a top-80 receiver, and Stribling at 2.15 was the steal of the second round. Deion Burks at 3.04 was the one genuine overpay. Three real assets from five picks with the best top-end fit in the draft.

02 CarlosMalagon A−

The best raw talent haul in the draft and the only team to land two immediate NFL starters — Carnell Tate as his team's WR1 and Kenyon Sadiq as an outright starting tight end. Three top-120 players is a league high. What keeps it from an A: he held picks 3, 6, 9 and 19 and paid full retail at every one, posting the second-worst value-per-pick among contenders. He also entered with a genuine superflex quarterback shortfall and never addressed it across six selections.

03 SmokeySupremacy A−

The best value-per-pick in the league and the third-largest talent haul, off seven selections — the most of any team that drafted efficiently. Jordyn Tyson and Emmett Johnson are both top-120, and Brazzell at 3.07 was the fourth-biggest value gain in the draft precisely because a season-ending knee injury scared the room off a player who costs you a year you weren't going to use. Kacmarek is the best remaining path at tight end. The honest mark against it: zero drafted rookies are NFL depth-chart starters, and Klubnik at 2.07 was the single largest reach on this board by value — a defensible bet on superflex scarcity, but a bet, and it came one pick after Kaytron Allen and Mike Washington were still there.

10 raidersfantasy B−

A tale of two drafts. Jadarian Price at 1.05 was close to perfect — a top-65 back who is already an NFL starter, landing on the one roster in the league with a genuine running back shortfall. Omar Cooper was fine. The middle rounds got aggressive: Drew Allar at 2.06 carried the steepest cost against the board, an unranked quarterback taken about a round ahead of his market, with Koziol at 3.05 in similar territory. Both are real upside bets in a format that rewards quarterback depth — they just came at a premium, and seven picks landed him third-lowest in value per pick.
